# 1. Seizure-Safe Schools

Status: active since 2026. Flagship initiative.

## The gap
Minnesota law (Minn. Stat. 121A.24) has required every public and charter school to have a
seizure action plan and trained staff since the 2022-23 school year. Nobody checks. We
reviewed the public websites of 328 of the state's 329 regular school districts. 231 of them,
about 70 percent, post no seizure-specific plan a family or a substitute teacher could find.
The gap tracks district size: small districts, most of them rural, lack the nurse and the
administrative time to publish one. About half of Minnesota districts have no licensed
school nurse at all.

The full analysis, with interactive charts, is in
[The Data: Mapping the Gaps](../chapters/05-the-data-case-study/index.md). Look up any
district in [Find Your District](../find-your-district/index.md).

## What we are doing this year
- Contacting every no-plan district, in order of need, with a free drop-in packet: a seizure
  action plan template in four languages, board policy language, a first-aid poster.
- Asking each district one question once staff are back: do you now have a plan on file for
  each student with a seizure disorder and a trained adult at each school? A yes, with a
  count, is how we measure this initiative.
- Asking the Minnesota School Boards Association to cite 121A.24 in its model medication
  policy, the one most small districts adopt word for word. Today it does not.
- Adding a language layer: 25 no-plan districts have ten percent or more English learners,
  about 15,000 students. They get the Spanish, Hmong, and Somali templates first.
- Re-auditing all 328 districts in spring 2027 and publishing the before-and-after map.

## Districts that confirmed they have a plan
In September we asked every district we had contacted one question: is there a seizure
action plan on file for each student with a seizure disorder, and a trained adult at each
school? These districts wrote back yes. Most had plans all along and simply do not post
them online, which is why a public audit alone undercounts. Their entries in

## Check yourself
??? quiz "1. What share of Minnesota districts post a findable seizure plan?"
    About 30 percent (97 of 328). About 70 percent post none, most of them only a general
    medication policy that never mentions seizures.

??? quiz "2. What predicts whether a district posts a plan: being rural, or being small?"
    Being small. Once enrollment is in the model, rurality is no longer significant. The gap
    is a capacity problem, which is why small districts need the work done for them.

??? quiz "3. Does 'no plan found online' mean a district is breaking the law?"
    No. We measure public findability, not compliance. A district may have an internal plan.
    That is why we ask each district directly before counting anything.
