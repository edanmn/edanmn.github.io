# Someone is having a seizure right now

<div class="emergency-steps" markdown>

## 1. Look at the clock

Note the time it started. The length is what decides everything else.

## 2. Stay with them

Keep other people back. Move anything hard or sharp out of the way. Put something soft under
the head. Loosen anything tight around the neck.

## 3. Do not do these things

Do not hold them down. Do not put anything in their mouth. Do not give food, drink or
medicine until they are fully awake.

## 4. When the jerking stops, roll them onto their side

This keeps the airway clear. Stay with them until they are properly awake and know where they
are. That can take twenty minutes or more, and they may be confused or frightened.

</div>

## Call 911 if any of these are true

- The convulsive seizure has lasted more than 5 minutes
- A second seizure starts before they recover
- They are having trouble breathing, or their lips look blue or grey
- They are injured, or the seizure happened in water
- This is their first known seizure
- They do not wake up afterwards
- Their seizure action plan says to call

Most seizures stop on their own and do not need an ambulance. If you are unsure, call.

## Giving rescue medicine

Only if this person has rescue medicine prescribed to them, and only in the way their seizure
action plan says. Note the time you gave it.

## Afterwards, write it down while it is fresh

The time it started and stopped. What the movements looked like and where they began. Whether
they responded to you. Any injury. Whether rescue medicine was given and when. How long until
they were back to normal. A family and a neurologist use exactly these notes.
